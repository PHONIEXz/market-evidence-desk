A PRACTICAL READING CHECKLIST

What would make a brief dependable?

1. Open the original

Check title, publication date, publisher and whether the document is a staff view, a regulator recommendation or a company account.

2. Trace the claim

Follow the source to its linked research question. Look for supporting, contextual and conflicting statements before repeating a conclusion.

3. Mark what is unknown

Identify the jurisdiction, review date and unanswered parts. The absence of a live feed or issuer audit means current safety and price questions remain open.

WHERE THE DATA LIVES

Two Sanity layers, one review boundary.

Published source, question and claim documents form the structured graph shown across the public pages. The Sourcebook Knowledge Base organizes source material for retrieval by the AI agent. The two are related, but a Knowledge Base entry does not by itself approve a research conclusion.

What the agent must do

The server asks Sanity Context for both a GROQ query and a Knowledge Base read. It then returns the generated draft with a tool receipt and links only to sources in the published graph. Reviewers still need to inspect original passages and any Sourcebook issues.
